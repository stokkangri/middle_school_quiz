Two roads to the robot

A Driver Hub can drive the robot only after they share a road. Wi-Fi and Wi-Fi Direct wear the same paint. One road can reach the internet through a router. The other is a private hall that stops at the robot.

Who is who

The jobs stay the same even when the boxes change. A phone can do either job. A REV hub can do either job. The road does not care which shell you used.

Visitor · client

Driver side

The REV Driver Hub, or an Android phone running the FTC Driver Station app. It sits with the drivers. It visits the robot and asks it to move.

Library · server

Robot side

The REV Control Hub on the robot, or an Android phone running the FTC Robot Controller app. It stays put, holds the program, and answers the driver.

Which box is the robot?

On the private road the robot keeps a famous house number. The number depends on which box owns the road. The web door stays 8080.

Six words, drawn as a town

Path

Wi-Fi or Wi-Fi Direct is a hallway, a private road. The yellow dashes are the paint. The street sign is the name. Until you walk it, you do not know if google.com is at the end.

IP address

The house number. On the Control Hub’s own road it is 192.168.43.1. A router road hands out a different number, such as 192.168.1.42. That second number is only an example.

Port

A door on that house. Door 8080 is the robot’s web page, where a program book can be dropped off. Joystick messages use a different door so they are not mixed up with the web page.

File

A program is a book on a shelf. It does not float to the robot. Someone carries it along a path, to a house number, through a door, onto the robot’s shelf.

Client

A private house that goes visiting. The Driver Hub is a client. Your laptop is a client when it opens the robot’s page or google.com.

Server

A library that stays open and answers visitors. The robot is a server. google.com and github.com are servers too. They live on the internet road, not on the robot’s private road.

Quick check

You need github.com. Which road do you walk?

github.com is a server on the internet. Only the router road has a ramp to that town.

At a match, your Driver Hub should talk to your robot on a private road. Which sign is that?

Wi-Fi Direct is the private hall between your driver and your robot. You do not need the internet to drive.

The plaque says 192.168.43.1 and you want the robot’s web page. What else do you need?

192.168.43.1 is the house. 8080 is the web door. Together they look like 192.168.43.1:8080.

You can explain this to your team. Same paint, different road. Address, then door. Client visits, server answers.

The real numbers on a real team

  • 192.168.43.1 is the Control Hub’s house number on the network it creates itself. The Driver Station still calls this kind of link Wi-Fi Direct.
  • 192.168.49.1 is the usual house number when the robot side is an Android phone on Wi-Fi Direct.
  • Door 8080 is the web page for both. A browser opens http:// that address, a colon, and 8080.
  • On a normal router, the router assigns the house numbers. They will not be those famous ones. The internet may be available on that same road.
  • Join the robot’s network on a laptop and google.com often stops loading, even while the Wi-Fi icon looks fine. You are on the private road. Google’s library is on the other one. Leave that road, join the home router, and the internet comes back — you may also lose the robot until it is on that router too.
  • At many events the private road is the one you use, so the driver is tied to that robot and the venue’s internet is not required.
